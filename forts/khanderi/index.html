THE STORY BEHIND THE STONE

Why Khanderi
matters.

Shivaji Maharaj selected the island of Khanderi to fortify it to keep a check on the British trade route to Mumbai and northward ports. He tried building the fort in 1672, but the British were sceptical and tried to siege the fort. The Marathas retreated, only to return back in 1679. Under the leadership of Maynak Bhandari, 150 men were garrisoned on Khanderi to complete the fortification. The English warned the Marathas, who did not pay heed to them. The Marathas continued their work without hindrance. The British then deputed their warships to block the supplies to the Marathas and stop their work.The Marathas till then had improvised a lot…

Khanderi and the contest for coastal waters

Khanderi lies off the coast south of Mumbai. Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj’s forces occupied the island in 1679. Maynak Bhandari is associated with its early fortification. The position mattered to movement along the northern Konkan coast. Its occupation drew opposition from nearby maritime powers. Maratha defenders had to bring men and supplies across open water. The island became a lasting Maratha naval stronghold. Later confrontations included an East India Company attack in 1718. The lighthouse belongs to a later British phase of the island’s history. Khanderi reveals how a small island could carry large strategic importance.

Read full story ＋

Khanderi is a small island whose position gave it significance beyond its size. In 1679, forces of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj occupied and fortified it, with Maynak Bhandari associated with the undertaking. The island lay within a coastal world influenced by the Siddis, the English at Bombay and other regional powers. Establishing a Maratha position there therefore affected more than the island itself: it altered the balance around nearby sea routes.

The practical challenge was to maintain a garrison in a place reached only by water. Fortifying an island required the movement of workers, provisions and building material while opponents sought to prevent the position from becoming secure. Khanderi’s history consequently joins construction to naval operations. Its defensive value came from the combination of location, walls, boats and the people able to operate between shore and island.

Khanderi remained important after the lifetime of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj. An East India Company expedition against it in 1718 failed, demonstrating the continued importance of Maratha coastal bases in the eighteenth century. That later event belongs to a different political moment and should not be merged into the original campaign of 1679. The fort’s long history includes changing commanders and arrangements of authority, not a single uninterrupted episode under one individual.

The lighthouse visible today is a later addition from the British period. Distinguishing it from the earlier fortifications helps visitors read the island’s successive layers accurately. The modern name Kanhoji Angre Island commemorates the Maratha admiral, while the foundation of the fort is linked to Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj’s maritime policy. The site is an especially clear example of how strategic geography could make a modest area of rock an important part of a much larger system of coastal defence.
