THE STORY BEHIND THE STONE

Why Pratapgad
matters.

Shivaji Maharaj commissioned Pratapgad above the routes of the Jawali region. The meeting and conflict with Afzal Khan in 1659 made the site central to the early history of Maratha expansion.

The fort above the Par Pass ＋

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj commissioned Pratapgad in 1656 to strengthen control of the Par Pass and the surrounding region. The confrontation with Afzal Khan on 10 November 1659 made it a decisive place in the early growth of Maratha power. Understanding the fort’s position above the valleys helps explain its strategic purpose.

Upper and lower fortified sections provided different defensive positions on the hill. Gateways, bastions and the Bhavani Temple remain important points of interest. The fort’s military history and sacred associations should be distinguished: devotional traditions belong to the site’s living heritage, while claims about specific events require historical evidence.

Foundations of Swarajya

Pratapgad: a decisive encounter

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj commissioned Pratapgad in 1656. The fort guarded a strategic mountain landscape near the Par pass. Bijapur sent the commander Afzal Khan against the growing Maratha power. The two leaders met below the fort on 10 November 1659. Afzal Khan died during their violent confrontation. Fighting between the opposing forces followed the encounter. The Maratha victory strengthened Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj’s position. The episode made Pratapgad central to the memory of Swarajya. Its significance includes preparation, terrain and organised command. The fort preserves a history larger than the famous meeting alone.

Read full story ＋

Pratapgad was completed in 1656 under Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj’s direction, with Moropant Pingle overseeing its construction. Its location helped secure access between the uplands and the Konkan, including the approaches around the Par pass. The choice of position matters: the fort occupied a landscape of wooded slopes, narrow routes and commanding heights. Three years later this newly established stronghold became the setting for one of the best-known episodes in early Maratha history.

Afzal Khan, a senior commander of the Bijapur sultanate, advanced against Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj. A meeting was arranged below Pratapgad on 10 November 1659. The encounter turned violent and Afzal Khan was killed. Subsequent fighting ended in a Maratha victory. Accounts differ in their detailed presentation of the meeting, so a visitor guide should separate these well-established events from dramatic dialogue and reconstructed gestures that cannot be independently checked.

The result increased the standing of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj and altered the balance of the campaign. It also illustrates how a fort, its approaches and forces outside its walls could work together. The engagement cannot be explained only by the height of the masonry. Preparation, the placement of troops and knowledge of local terrain were equally important parts of the wider military setting.

Today the fort, its Bhavani temple and the commemorative landscape invite several kinds of attention. Visitors can honour the achievements of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj while also studying the physical organisation of the site and the different historical sources. Respect for the past is strengthened by that distinction: the human decisions and strategic setting are compelling without invented speeches, unsupported casualty totals or claims that every surviving structure dates to the same year.
